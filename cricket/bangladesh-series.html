1st Test · Wanderers

In SA v Bangladesh Tests since 2022, Bangladesh's batters have averaged 16.8 or less against every SA bowler type, and SA's batters have averaged lowest against Bangladesh's left-arm spin.

The head-to-head match-ups are the clearest pattern we have, but the sample is small, so the size sits next to every number. Recent records need their opposition beside them. The Wanderers data is old: the last Test there in our database was played in March 2023. South Africa named a 15-player squad on 2 Nov; Bangladesh have not, so their bowler types come from a provisional list.

Written 2026-11-03, before the match.

Finding 1

SA's edge with the ball: every bowler type has taken Bangladesh wickets cheaply

In head-to-head Tests since 2022, SA's two left-arm orthodox spinners did most of the work: 34 wickets from 1111 balls at an average of 14.8 against Bangladesh's batters. SA's right-arm pace was cheaper still at 10.3, but from only 599 balls. Off-spin: 16.8. Small sample, and the figures are not split by venue, so home and away conditions are mixed together.

Small sample 4 head-to-head Tests; left-arm orthodox is two bowlers.

Sample. 4 head-to-head Tests (Mar 2022 to Oct 2024); 1111 + 599 + 485 balls. Two bowlers each for left-arm orthodox and off-spin, three for right-arm pace. Small sample. Bowler types are known only for players in the squads (SA's 15 named on 2 Nov; Bangladesh provisional), so balls from anyone else are left out.

Finding 2

SA's soft spot with the bat: Bangladesh's left-arm orthodox spin

SA's batters have averaged 27.8 against Bangladesh's left-arm orthodox spin (22 wickets, 1052 balls). That is well below their 45.3 against Bangladesh's right-arm pace and 58.2 against off-spin. Two left-arm spinners account for that figure. Same small head-to-head sample since 2022, with conditions mixed. It describes the past, not what will happen in this Test.

Small sample 4 head-to-head Tests; left-arm orthodox is two bowlers.

Sample. 4 head-to-head Tests (Mar 2022 to Oct 2024); 1052 + 1542 + 1163 balls. Left-arm orthodox comes from 2 bowlers, so one bowler's form can move it a lot. Small sample. Bowler types come from ICC profiles; Bangladesh's squad is not announced yet, so their list is provisional.

Finding 3

SA's record since 2024 is stronger, but compare the opposition

Since 2024, SA have won 14 of 20 Tests. Against top-8 sides the record is 10-5-1. Against Bangladesh and Zimbabwe it is 4-0-0. Bangladesh are 9-11-1 overall and 6-9-1 against the top 8, including 4-0-0 against Pakistan. SA's bowling average of 23.7 compares with Bangladesh's 31.2. Both sides' lists of opponents differ, so read the records side by side, not as a ranking.

Sample. 20 and 21 Tests. The third SA v Australia Test (Cape Town) is not in Cricsheet yet, so SA's form ends on 18 Oct 2026. Opponent tiers: top-8 = India, Australia, England, South Africa, New Zealand, Pakistan, Sri Lanka, West Indies.

Finding 4

At the Wanderers, toss winners have batted first, and first-innings totals are the highest

Across 19 Wanderers Tests in our data, the side batting first averaged 298.0, against 227.0 in the second innings. In 18 of those 19 Tests the toss winner chose to bat, and those sides won 11. Across all Tests since 2022, batting first shows no such edge (27 won, 29 lost). The last Wanderers Test in our data is from March 2023.

Sample. 19 Tests from 2003 to 2023. Only 1 toss winner chose to field, so bat and field cannot be compared at this venue. The pitch may have changed since March 2023.

What to watch by phase

  • New ball (overs 0-20). Since 2024, this has been Bangladesh's weakest phase with the bat: an average of 25.1 and 74.2% dot balls. SA's batters averaged 37.6 in this phase at the Wanderers (59 wickets).
  • Middle (overs 20-80). Since 2024, Bangladesh have averaged 27.6 here and SA 33.0. At the Wanderers, SA averaged 33.1 over 187 wickets. The phase table does not split by bowler type, so it does not show when the spinners bowled.
  • Second new ball (overs 80+). Since 2024, SA's batters scored fastest in this phase (4.24 runs per over, average 36.1). Bangladesh averaged 31.9. At the Wanderers, though, SA averaged only 25.2 in this phase (65 wickets).

What the data cannot say

  • Bowling averages here are per bowler wicket from the scorecard; run outs are not bowler wickets, and Bavuma's retirement is not counted as a wicket; he finished 68 not out.

Short samples are noisy: a figure on 60-200 balls carries a standard error of roughly +/-9 to +/-16 runs for a batter and +/-9 to +/-16 runs for a bowler (per-ball spread 1.15 and 1.15 runs, Test baseline pool). Method and sources

Where every number comes from

Ball-by-ball data from Cricsheet is loaded into a local SQLite database. The script python -m src.analysis.wanderers computes the dossier figures and python -m src.analysis.review_ban1 the Review tab; this page is generated from the same database and the build stops if a number in the written copy differs from the one computed.

Definitions

  • Phases of a Test innings, by over number: new ball overs 0-20, middle overs 20-80, second new ball overs 80+.
  • Match-up average: runs off the bat divided by wickets credited to the bowler. Strike rate in the tables is runs per 100 balls.
  • Phase average: runs divided by dismissals in that phase. Runs per over uses legal balls. Dot % is the share of legal balls with no run.
  • Bowling average: runs conceded by bowlers (off the bat, wides and no-balls) per bowler wicket.
  • Opponent tiers: top-8 means India, Australia, England, South Africa, New Zealand, Pakistan, Sri Lanka or West Indies. Other opponents are grouped as other.
  • Wanderers: Johannesburg grounds with Wanderers in the name (Cricsheet spells it several ways).
