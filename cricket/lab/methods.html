Methods

How every number is made

Built from Himanish Ganjoo's public primer, "T20 Metrics: A Primer" (2026). The primer does not publish its fitted constants or its Appendix A, so everything here is re-fitted on our own data. Where we had to choose, it says so.

Data

Pro data: Cricsheet ball-by-ball JSON, men's matches from 2015, 20 overs. T20Is are limited to matches between the 12 ICC full members (associate games are a different population). Super overs are left out. "Clean" matches (no rain rule, no reduced overs, a result) are used to fit the curve, par and win probability; every match is scored.

Last Man Stands data: the public LMS live-scoring feed (FixtureState) for Johannesburg/Sandton leagues, cached once per match. Players and opposition teams are opaque ids. The extras rule is inferred from the data: the first wide or no-ball in an over is 1 run and re-bowled; a later one in the same over is 3 runs and uses up a ball. With that rule the ball log sums exactly to the official total in the share of matches shown above; the curve, par and win probability are fitted on those matches only.

Par scores (nested shrinkage)

tree: global → competition (LMS: division) → season → ground par_child = w · mean_child + (1 − w) · par_parent, w = n / (n + k) k = s²_within / s²_between s²_within = pooled variance of totals around their own child means s²_between = var(child means) − mean(s²_within / n_j), floored at 25

The primer describes this in words. Our choices: the method-of-moments correction on s²_between (switchable; held-out RMSE with and without it is on the validation page); k is estimated only where a parent has ≥ 3 children and ≥ 10 innings, otherwise the k pooled across that level, otherwise 8. T20Is use the same tree as leagues (competition → season → ground) instead of the primer's host-country level, because Cricsheet does not record the host country. Par is in-sample for the published tables; the held-out tests refit it on 70% of matches.

Resource curve

R(b, w, λ) = R0 · F(w) · λ^(n_w + 1) · (1 − exp(−b / (β(w) · λ^n_w))) n_w = n0 · F(w), F(w) = 1 + f1·w + f2·w² + f3·w³, β(w) = β0 · (1 + g1·w + g2·w² + g3·w³) b = legal balls left, w = wickets down; R = 0 when b = 0 or the side is all out λ solves R(B, 0, λ) = par (bisection on [0.7, 2.5]; λ < 1 allowed)

Fitted by weighted least squares on the mean runs still to come in every (b, w) cell of first innings (weights n / variance, cells with fewer than 10 balls dropped), with penalties keeping F and β decreasing and runs-to-come falling when a wicket falls. n0 is then fitted on matches in the top quarter of par, re-solving λ for each candidate. Fitted values:

RAA and WAA

batter: RAA = runs off the bat − E[runs | state], WAA = E[a batter is out | state] − (striker out) bowler: RAA = E[runs charged | state] − runs charged, WAA = (bowler's wicket) − E[bowler's wicket | state] state = (innings, overs left, wickets down, par in the 1st innings or target in the 2nd)

The primer uses gradient-boosted trees on these four features, deliberately allowed to overfit. T20 Lab uses the transparent alternative: average outcomes in cells of (innings, overs left, wickets down, par/target band of 10 runs), each cell shrunk towards its coarser parent with n / (n + 30). Wides are not balls faced; no-balls count as faced but carry no RAA expectation; a batter out off a wide or no-ball is charged the dismissal. For bowlers, wides and no-balls count as runs conceded with no expectation. A run-out non-striker is charged −1 WAA.

Impact (runs added)

Impact = runs + R(b − 1, w + wicket, λ) − R(b, w, λ) (b unchanged on a wide / no-ball) striker: runs off the bat + ΔR (+ the wicket's cost if he is out); run-out non-striker: the wicket's cost bowler: −(runs charged + ΔR + his wicket's cost); run-out wicket cost → fielding; byes, leg-byes → extras

Same formula in both innings, with the match λ. We do not cap the chase (the primer's optional cap) and do not publish opponent-adjusted Impact.

Win probability, WPA, leverage, clutch

1st innings: r = (S + R(b, w, λ) + 1) / R(B, 0, λ) 2nd innings: r = R(b, w, λ) / N, N = runs needed P = 1 / (1 + exp(−(a + n · ln r))) primer: a = 0, n = 6 WPA = P_after − P_before (batting side); bowler gets −WPA (run-out share to fielding) Leverage L = P(next legal ball is a six) − P(next legal ball is a wicket for 0); LI = L / mean L of that innings Clutch = WPA / mean LI − Σ WPA_i / max(LI_i, 0.1) (≥ 300 balls with ≥ 50 at LI > 1.5)

The primer's rendered first-innings ratio shows a "+1" that its prose does not explain; we keep it because it makes the end of the first innings meet the start of the chase exactly (with equal n). a and n are fitted by maximum likelihood per innings; the primer's n = 6 is shown alongside. The shift a is Platt scaling, added because n = 6 alone missed our calibration target (expected calibration error ≤ 0.03) on held-out matches. Clutch and leverage-weighted Impact are T20 Lab definitions: the primer marks those sections "in preparation".

Limits

  • Ball-by-ball results only: no ball tracking, field settings, shot or bowler type. Context beyond the scoreboard (who bowls next, pitch wear) is not in the numbers.
  • Every figure is a description of past matches with sampling noise. Small samples are labelled; a season of 100 balls is noisy.
  • Par conflates conditions with team strength (a weak home batting side lowers its ground's par), as the primer notes.
  • Player names are as Cricsheet spells them; two players with the same short name in one competition would be merged.
  • LMS: the extras rule is inferred, and some ball logs do not add up to the official total; those matches are scored but not used for fitting.
