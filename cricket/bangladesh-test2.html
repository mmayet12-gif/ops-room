Addition 1 · Centurion

Over blocks by pace and spin (proxy)

Sample: 20 Tests at SuperSport Park in the database (2005 to 2024), 32,799 legal balls and 641 bowler wickets. Cricsheet has no day or session markers, so the blocks are proxies: a Test's legal balls are numbered across the match, a day is 90 overs and a block is 30 overs (exact only on an uninterrupted match; rain, bad light and innings ending mid-session move the real sessions). Coverage limit: only 5,382 of the 32,799 balls (16%) and 135 of the 641 wickets (21%) are by a bowler with a known type (ICC profile; typed balls come from 11 of those Tests, 2014 to 2024); the rest are not guessed and sit outside the pace and spin columns. So 'pace's share of wickets' is shown two ways: of the typed wickets (the direction of the coverage bias is unknown), and as a range of all wickets (low: only typed pace wickets count; high: every untyped wicket counts as pace). Pace or spin cells under 200 balls are hidden; a share needs 10+ wickets. Runs per over = (runs off the bat + wides + no-balls) / legal balls x 6; balls per wicket = legal balls / bowler wickets (run outs excluded).

Addition 2 · Centurion

Over in the spell, pace at Centurion

Sample: 761 overs (4,536 balls) by pace bowlers of known type at SuperSport Park, in 10 Tests. A spell is a run of one bowler's overs where each follows his last after exactly one other bowler's over; the overnight break does not end it (no day markers). Expected economy and wickets are for the same format, phase (new ball, middle, second new ball), bowler type and venue (venue factor included), so a gap is against a typical ball in that context at this ground. Buckets under 200 balls are hidden. The rough SE on wickets is the square root of expected wickets: a gap inside it is noise.

How long South Africa's quicks bowl their spells (the pace-typed bowlers in the 2 Nov squad, every Test in the pool, home and away; Bavuma, one spell in the pool, is left out)

A bowler needs 10 spells to be ranked. The spell rule does not break at the overnight gap, so a spell that carries into the next morning counts as one, which lengthens the longest spells; the median is the safer column.

Addition 3 · Both sides

New batters: the first 20 balls by bowler type

What it measures: how often a batter is dismissed in the first 20 balls he faces in an innings, against pace and against spin, compared with the dismissals the set-batter expectation gives for the same balls (format, phase, bowler type, venue). Out per 100 = dismissals / balls x 100. The expectation does not know the batter is new, so the benchmark rows (all Test batters, same balls) are the yardstick: across all bowlers of known type the benchmark gap is +0.5 per 100 balls v pace and +0.7 v spin; against South Africa bowlers +1.0 and +1.0; against Bangladesh bowlers -0.2 and +0.7. Read each batter against the benchmark row of his own table, not against zero. Shrinkage: the gap is also shown shrunk, (outs - expected outs) / (balls + 240) x 100, which pulls a thin sample toward zero. Ranking: rows need 60+ balls; smaller rows are shown with their n and never ranked. The all-ball columns give the same batter's shrunk out rate over every ball he has faced against that group (matchups.matrix), for context. Coverage limit: only balls from bowlers of known type (an ICC profile) count, and the typed bowlers come from only some teams, for most batters a small slice of their first-20 balls; the coverage line under each table gives the share and the teams, computed. The 'all bowlers of known type' and 'v one team only' tables differ only by the typed bowlers of other teams (the coverage line names the teams). Pool: Tests in the database (SA, Bangladesh and England since 2022, and SA home Tests back to 2003); nothing adjusts for the opposition's strength.

Method and sources

Where every number comes from

Ball-by-ball data from Cricsheet is loaded into a local SQLite database. This page is generated from the output of two scripts, python -m src.analysis.centurion_refresh and python -m src.analysis.wanderers --venue centurion; the build stops if either differs from the reviewed document, or if any table row from them is missing from the page or the page has a table they do not have.

Generated by python -m src.analysis.centurion_refresh. The 2nd Test is at SuperSport Park, Centurion, 23-27 Nov 2026. The 1st Test (Wanderers, 15-19 Nov; SA won by 9 wickets) and the SA v Australia Cape Town Test (27-31 Oct) are not in Cricsheet, so they enter only through their official scorecards (ESPNcricinfo, read 20 Nov 2026): results, innings totals and wickets, and bowling figures by type. The 1st Test adds nothing ball-by-ball. Additions 1 to 3 below use only the Cricsheet baseline pool (every ball in the database). Descriptive only: no forecast of the 2nd Test.
